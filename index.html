Your Mac, at a glance, in the menu bar.

SysDial shows processor, memory, disk, network and battery as live numbers in the menu bar, warns you before things slow down, and stays light while it does.

Everything you check, in one place

Pick what you want in the menu bar and in what order. Click it to open a panel of cards with the details.

System

  • Processor with every efficiency and performance core
  • Memory with pressure, swap and what it's used for
  • GPU load and video memory
  • Disk space, the way Finder counts it
  • Battery health, cycles, power draw and time left

Network

  • Download and upload speed with your public and local IP
  • Data transferred today, this week and in your billing month
  • Internet quality: latency, jitter, packet loss and outages

Apps

  • Top processes by processor and memory
  • Watched apps: follow one app's CPU and memory for days, with a memory alert
  • Leftover dev processes that keep running after a terminal or editor closes

Alerts

  • A runaway app keeping the processor busy
  • High CPU load and memory pressure
  • Low disk space and internet outages

Your menu bar, your way

Switch modules on and off and drag them into the order you like, or keep only the icon. Try it here, it works the same way in the app.

Nine themes, one tap

Every theme recolours the panel and the window. Pick one in Settings and it changes straight away.

On your desktop, and in your shortcuts

Put the numbers where you already look, or hand them to your own automations.

  • Small widget shows processor and memory.
  • Medium widget adds disk and battery.
  • Shortcuts can read processor, memory, disk and battery in your automations.
  • Siri can open SysDial for you.

Right-click the desktop, choose Edit Widgets and search for SysDial. macOS refreshes widgets about every 15 minutes.

Light enough to forget it's running

With live numbers in the menu bar and the panel closed, SysDial uses under 1% of the processor and under 100 MB of memory, even while watching several apps. Measured on a MacBook Pro with Apple M3 Pro, running on battery. It reads the same system counters as Activity Monitor and does nothing in the background that it doesn't need to.

Pro opens the window

The menu bar and the panel are the quick look. Pro adds a full window for when you need to know what happened and why.

  1. The panel’s cards plus load average and fans, together in one window you can leave open on a second display.

  2. Search, sort, reveal and quit any process. Apps you watch get their own cards with processor, memory, the day’s memory peak and an alert level. SysDial also looks for leftover dev processes, like terraform, node and python, that kept running after a terminal closed.

  3. Insights explain what is slowing your Mac, like swap in use and the apps using the most, with a button to free up memory. Alerts keep a seven-day log of runaway apps, processor, memory, disk and internet or DNS outages.

  4. Processor, memory, network and internet latency on one timeline, with alerts marked where they happened. Switch between 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days or any period you pick, and export what you see to CSV.

  5. Each watched app gets its own chart of memory and processor, with every launch marked and your memory alert drawn as a line, so you can see exactly when it crossed.

  6. Runaway processes and load peaks are listed with the time, how long they lasted and which app caused them, so you don’t have to hunt through a chart for them.

  7. App caches, logs, simulator caches and npm or Terraform caches, sized and ticked for you. Items go to the Trash, so you can put them back. The Spotlight index can be reset when it grows too large.

Your data stays on your Mac

SysDial has no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. Readings, history and the apps you watch are stored only on your Mac. It goes online for two things: to show your public IP, which you can turn off, and to check internet quality.
