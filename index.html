It remembers, so you don’t have to

The menu bar shows now. The Pro window shows what happened, when it happened and which app was behind it. Scroll to see how it works.

  1. Processor, memory, network and internet latency on one timeline, with alerts marked where they happened. Switch between 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days or any period you pick, and export what you see to CSV.

  2. Runaway processes and load peaks are listed with the time, how long they lasted and which app caused them, so you don’t have to hunt through a chart for them.

  3. Insights explain what is slowing your Mac, like swap in use and the apps using the most, with a button to free up memory. Alerts keep a seven-day log of runaway apps, processor, memory, disk and internet or DNS outages.

  4. Click the eye next to any process and SysDial follows it, up to 12 apps. Each gets a card with live processor and memory, the day’s memory peak and an alert level, and you set the memory limit that raises the alert. Search, sort, reveal and quit from the same list. SysDial also finds leftover dev processes, like terraform, node and python, that kept running after a terminal closed.

  5. Each followed app gets its own chart of memory and processor on the History page, with every launch marked and your memory limit drawn as a line, so you can see exactly when it crossed.

  6. The panel’s cards plus load average and fans, together in one window you can leave open on a second display.

  7. App caches, logs, simulator caches and npm or Terraform caches, sized and ticked for you. Items go to the Trash, so you can put them back. The Spotlight index can be reset when it grows too large.

On your desktop, and in your shortcuts

Put the numbers where you already look, or hand them to your own automations.

  • Small widget shows processor and memory.
  • Medium widget adds disk and battery.
  • Shortcuts can read processor, memory, disk and battery in your automations.
  • Siri can open SysDial for you.

Right-click the desktop, choose Edit Widgets and search for SysDial. macOS refreshes widgets about every 15 minutes.

Light enough to forget it's running

With live numbers in the menu bar and the panel closed, SysDial uses under 1% of the processor and under 100 MB of memory, even while watching several apps. Measured on a MacBook Pro with Apple M3 Pro, running on battery. It reads the same system counters as Activity Monitor and does nothing in the background that it doesn't need to.

Your data stays on your Mac

SysDial has no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. Readings, history and the apps you watch are stored only on your Mac. It goes online for two things: to show your public IP, which you can turn off, and to check internet quality.
